# World

## Setting [DECIDED 2026-09-26: unique and invented, modern technology, not cyberpunk]

A town on the east coast of an island like Vancouver Island: Hutch's own town, generalized, with features from the general coastal BC feeling. [DECIDED]

The brief: a contentious time in history at the end of an empire, in a rowdy bar that's dingy, lived in, and as debaucherous as it really is and no more. [DECIDED]

Three routes:

1. **Real history with a dated end:** Vienna in November 1918, Moscow in December 1991, or Hong Kong in June 1997.
2. **An invented world built on real history**, the way Disco's Revachol is. **Claude's recommendation.** Steal texture from one of the real ones and keep the freedom. Table 28 gets to stay table 28, and "no caricatures" is easier to keep when nobody's real political side is in play.
3. **An invented version of now.**

Whatever the route, a failing currency fits (Weimar in 1923, Russia in 1992). So does a dated public end that everyone counts down to.

## Day 1: the test shift and the rats

**Decided:**

- It's a Wednesday: a test shift for the new bouncer. It's the prologue and the tutorial.
- There's a rat problem, a nod to the kill-rats opening of a lot of RPGs. It's a bit light-hearted at first, but it can bite him long term.
- He finds out the rats are regulars too. Depending on how he reacts, he can side with the rats.

**Proposed:**

- **"Rat" also means informant.** Siding with the rats puts him in league with the one faction that hears everything, in a bar where the kids' first rule is don't be a rat.
- **Rats leave a sinking ship.** If he's allied with them, they're gone the night before the event. The tutorial's joke becomes the ending's omen.
- **The long-term bite is concrete.** If he poisons them, a dead rat rots in the wall and stinks for a week. The health inspector is on the calendar. If he ignores them, their numbers double.
- **The first three days teach the game.** Wednesday teaches the door, the floor and closing on a slow night. Thursday teaches daylight. Friday is the first real shift. The first full playable build is these three days.

## The narrator [OPEN: Hutch asked "what is it?"; Claude's proposal below]

- Someone is telling this story at a bar, years later. The telling is in the past tense and third person ("He started on a Wednesday."). The voices in his head speak in the present tense and second person, like Disco's. So the text runs on two clocks.
- The foreshadowing lives on the teller's clock, because the teller already knows how it ends.
- Who is telling it, and to whom, is revealed at the end, and it depends on the playthrough. It might be one of the kids, grown up, the last of table 28, or the bouncer himself. If it's him, "he" becomes "I" in the last scene.
- **The rule that keeps the foreshadowing honest:** the event is fixed, but what it does to everyone isn't. The narrator can promise the event because it happens in every playthrough.
- Hutch voices the narrator. [PROPOSED]
