A reputation and good and evil(not ai simplistic as that binary) system that conditions the player and makes some things possible and others not, but in a more thoughtful and fine way than simple stats. ## The premise

- **Working title: The Bouncer.** [DECIDED] The name is already on a Square game from 2001; see Legal in `04_PRODUCTION.md`.
- **The protagonist is the new bouncer.** [DECIDED] Disco's cop had a badge and no self. A doorman has judgment and no badge, and his whole job is deciding who gets in.
- **His head works like his door.** [PROPOSED] He collects many voices, but only five fit in the room at once, so letting one in means throwing one out. Jung's *Seven Sermons to the Dead* opens with the dead at his door, back from Jerusalem, asking to be let in.
- **Implicated, never absolved.** [PROPOSED] He lets in the man who does the damage, and he puts people out into the rain. The job implicates him every night, and the game never lets him off.

## What we keep from Disco Elysium [PROPOSED]

- Voices that interrupt, disagree and have personalities.
- Checks where failure is content, often the best content.
- Ideas that take time to become yours.
- A protagonist with a self to recover and invent.
- Comedy and grief in the same scene.

## What we change [PROPOSED]

- **The character sheet is relational.** No skill levels, only the relationships between the voices in the room (`02_SYSTEMS.md`).
- **Skills have sources.** They come from people, texts and retold stories, not from points.
- **Morality is codes, witnesses and rumor**, not a meter.
- **One building, thirty nights**, instead of a district over a week.
- **Two tenses.** The narrator speaks from years later; the voices speak in the present.
- **No caricatures** (below).

## Writing rules

- **Debauchery as it really is, and no more.** The boredom, the sticky floors, the same songs every night. [DECIDED]
- **No one makes it out of the life unscathed.** Nobody who works in the bar gets to be the clean exception; the sober-bartender trope is out. [DECIDED 2026-09-26]
- **Stakes.** The writing has to make it feel like something is at risk. [DECIDED]
- **Serious in tone.** Humor, yes. Quips, never. Hutch has no patience for quippy, "Marvel-style" game writing. [DECIDED: Hutch's standing taste]
- **No caricatures.** Hutch's critique of Disco is that it gives some ideologies an inner voice and others a caricature. Here every faction gets its best voice, including the ones the game thinks are wrong, and the game holds their adherents dear as people. In Hutch's words, "separate ideology from the adherent." [PROPOSED, built on Hutch's critique]
- **Every voice is wrong in its own way.** [PROPOSED]
- **A failed check has to be as good as a pass.** [PROPOSED]
- **Nobody should need to know the math to feel it.** [PROPOSED]
- **Every choice changes something someone says later.** The minimum bar for reactivity. [PROPOSED]
- **Bad calls haunt without becoming a death spiral.** [DECIDED; the mechanism is PROPOSED in `02_SYSTEMS.md`]
