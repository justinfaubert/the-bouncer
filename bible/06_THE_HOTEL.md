## The building [PROPOSED]

An old three-storey wooden hotel on the east-coast highway, across the road from the strait. It was built when the town had a cannery and a mill, as a hotel with a dining room. Now the dining room is the pub, the rooms upstairs rent by the month, and the cold beer and wine store keeps the lights on.

- **Outside:** a gravel lot off the highway, puddles all winter, a smokers' bench, the dumpster in the alley. Across the highway is a seawall and the water, with the mainland mountains when it's clear. The neon sign has letters out.
- **Name:** **The Sovereign Hotel**, called "the Sov." It's named for the empire back when that meant something, and the name gets heavier as the thirty days run down. Alternatives: the Breakwater, the Kingfisher.

## The four doors [PROPOSED; the loose rule is DECIDED]

The loose rule is the design. The main door is his; the other three are how the night gets around him.

1. **The main door.** Off the gravel lot, through a small vestibule with a cigarette-burned ledge. His stool, the ID scanner, the clicker for the count. Everyone who comes in legitimately passes him.
2. **The hotel door.** A hallway door to the hotel's lobby and stairs. Residents come down to drink without passing him, and the night clerk props it open to hear the room. It's also his way upstairs to bed.
3. **The deck door.** Out to the smoking deck on the water side. Locked when the deck closes, and propped with a folded coaster every night after. Things come in this way, people go out this way to fight, and people come back in who were put out the front.
4. **The kitchen door.** Out the back of the kitchen to the alley and the dumpster. Deliveries and kegs. The cook leaves it open for air. The rats use it too.

Every night he decides how hard to enforce the other three. Locking them properly costs goodwill with residents, smokers and the cook; leaving them loose lets the room fill with people he never read.

## The pub [PROPOSED]

- **The bar** runs along the inside wall, with a brass rail and the carpet worn to the backing in front of it. Behind it are the till, the door to the beer and wine store, and the trapdoor to the keg room.
- **The floor:** sturdy old dining tables and cheap stools, a pool table with a lean, a dartboard, a touchscreen jukebox that streams, and two TVs showing hockey and the news. The lottery screen by the bar that table 28 plays all afternoon.
- **Table 28** is the big round table by the front window. The tables still carry their numbers from the old dining room's floor plan, so they skip and don't add up, and 28 is the one that survived. It has the best view of the door and the TV, and they've sat there longer than anyone on staff has worked there.
- **The back hall:** past the pool table to the bathrooms, a dead payphone still on the wall, and the hotel door at the end. The men's, the women's, and one single locked washroom where most of what happens in bathrooms happens. Half the plot is in the bathroom checks.
- **The kitchen** is behind the bar: a fryer, a flat-top, and the walk-in cooler.
- **The keg room** is in the basement, under the trapdoor. It's an old stone foundation close to the water, damp all year.

## The rats [PROPOSED]

They live in the foundation and come up through the keg room, along the pipes to the kitchen, and inside the wall behind table 28. So they hear everything table 28 says, which fits the informant double meaning. On a quiet Wednesday you can hear them in the wall.

## The rooms upstairs [PROPOSED]

About twenty rooms over two floors. The residents are the hotel's recurring cast: monthly tenants on pensions and disability, a work crew in for a contract, the occasional traveller, and whoever the night clerk lets have a room for cash. The bouncer's room comes with the job, and the rent comes off his pay, which gives the owner a hold on him.

Don't name or write the residents before Wednesday plays end to end. For now they're roles.
