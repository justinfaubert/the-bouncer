### Where it goes

What Hutch decided about the skeleton is in `05_LOG.md` (DECIDED, 14 October) and folded into `11_SKELETON.md`. The build fixes (the jukebox, the dice and their tray, the camera) went to The game's future form. The rest of the material goes here:

**The door craft** goes to `11_SKELETON.md`, "Hands on: the fight". How fights start, and the presence that stops most of them, are now the fight's first layer: standing tall, the big hand on the shoulder, irreverence and getting real serious. Aikido's locks and Krav Maga's blows are the two ways through once it goes physical, each with its own voice and consequences. The choke is in, as the skill most likely to kill a man by accident. Bucking protocol to step into someone else's fight is one of the game's hard questions.

**What he wears** goes to "Money, the strip and what he wears". No SECURITY shirts on this island; in the draft there's a box of them in Gary's office, and wearing one is the mainland mistake. Long hair and a mala are a no-no on most doors, and they say how loose he's willing to be. The draft gives him both, and the player decides each night whether the hair is tied and the mala stays on.

**Island Time** goes to "The month". It's the slower pace newcomers to the island are told to get used to. Once in Tofino it was literal: the electric clocks ran slow when the area's power ran islanded from the grid ([CBC](https://amp.cbc.ca/news/canada/british-columbia/tofino-b-c-where-even-the-clocks-are-on-island-time-1.3233753)). In the game, the island runs late against the bar's fast clock and the phones' mainland time, and after the end island time is all there is.

**Drinking on shift** goes to "Drink" and "Temptations". A drink on shift is neither a crime nor a failing in the game. It's a position in the room: the prude is only mildly respected, and a drink here and there puts him on their side of the bar. Gary feeds the staff shots when he's in. The office downstairs and the joint on the deck are there too.

**Table 28's Sundays** go to "The people": golfing or recovering. Some Sundays one of them comes in alone, to be somebody the table never lets him see. The range moves from table 28 to the vets.

**The games** go to "Games in the bar": pool, darts, poker after close (illegal for money, played anyway), and the meat draw every week. Farkle replaces crib.

**The stories:**

1. **The owner and the women** belong to Gary Van Dusen. It's power, money and complicity, told without titillation. The women are people choosing under pressure, not props. The bouncer sees the pattern (who gets away with what) before he understands it, and what he does about it is a hard question.
2. **The brawl in the lot** happens on the mill's last shift and its wake, day 24, the biggest night of the month at the door. A strange drug is going round, and young men are losing the only future the town had. He pulls the fight apart one group after another. A young man is glassed and needs thirty-one stitches. He's swung at and not hit, and he's respected because he takes care of people. Seven police SUVs come half an hour after it's over. The fight's seconds run one pair after another.
3. **The regular who cracked his head** is about overserving: "a dirty not-so-secret of any successful bar, especially a roadhouse. But its done with sense usually." It brings in whose drink it was, the first-aid kit, the ambulance, and what Gary says about the till.
4. **The cooks and the prime rib** become a strand. Fridays are prime rib night: the thin slices, the rest taken home. If the bouncer makes a friend of the supplier's driver, the driver checks what was ordered against what came and what was left the next day (the supplier is fictional in the game). The cooks get caught when they get greedy and run out at half past six. If he has terms with the rats, they show him where the rest goes.
5. **The rugby boys, and the young men** are a returning group on Saturdays after a match: yahoos who fight each other and make a scene. Then there are the town's young men in general, with the mill closing. Mettle, women and a rage with nowhere to go.
6. **The club** is fictional in the game, because the real one is quick to sue over its name and marks (Toys "R" Us, Saks and Alexander McQueen, Dillard's). It's never there in force, the higher-ups dress like anyone, and the try-hard prospects wear the club's number on their necks. They need an intelligent hand.
7. **The vets** become the old soldier (Hutch's Jim, renamed in the game) and the regulars who served. They're stoic, take-no-shit, some eccentric and some deep in PTSD. The range on Sundays is theirs, and so is a long table at the diner in the morning.
8. **The weekend damage** goes to the room's states. By Sunday the paper towel dispenser is in the garbage can and someone has pissed under the sink, and it stays that way until somebody deals with it.
9. **The man at the side door** is one man, never a people. Hutch: "There are racial tensions there that could/should be explored with nuance and intelligence and compassion." The town's Sikh families go back to the island's mills. Paldi, near Duncan, was a lumber town founded by Mayo Singh in 1917, and its gurdwara, built in 1919, still stands ([The Canadian Encyclopedia](https://thecanadianencyclopedia.ca/en/article/paldi)). So there's a Sikh regular at the Sov drawn as fully and as flawed as anyone, and there are Sikh men at the mill's last shift. The test is the room afterwards: what table 28 says, what the rugby boys say, what he writes in the incident log, and what he lets stand at the bar.
10. **The sucker punch** starts with the man he bans for dealing coke and starting fights, who is a server's boyfriend. Weeks later the banned man comes in late on a busy night with a friend, and the bouncer lets it slide. He's leaning on the bar with his arms folded, a stance with an opening in it. The friend comes up to the bar with a look, and that's the punch. The old soldier gets to his feet, too old and too slow, and gets his eyebrow split. The kid goes out the side door.
11. **Sex and outside drink** go to the bathroom checks and the lot: the knock, the flashlight, or the look away.
12. **The bookkeeper** sits in Gary's office, at the other desk during the interview: a drunk who skims cheques, a control freak working Gary. In the draft she writes his first cheque short.
