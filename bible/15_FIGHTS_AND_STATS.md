# Fights and stats

*Written 23 November 2026 from Hutch's answers to the survey's open questions (`12_FROM_THE_DOOR.md`, 23 November). Everything here is PROPOSED unless marked DECIDED. What's built is marked (built) and listed at the end.*

Hutch's answers, in short (DECIDED, 23 November):

- **The feel.** Frozen seconds for story fights, live play for the ring and side fights, all by hand gestures. A few story fights open in frozen seconds and tip into live fighting. "Robust, fun, feel impactful and have great feeling, intuitive controls."
- **The ring.** The men he fights "should look and act like they walked out of the mid 19th century or older," with a real history behind the club, and they get stranger as the days go and reality goes.
- **Stats.** Fighting and the rest of play raise sub-stats of their own that cross thresholds into general skill. The whole system made "more relevant, useful to the player and consequential," with deeper story integration.
- **Repeatable, never identical** replaces the skeleton's "no repeatable jobs."
- **His story of the ogre on the deck** goes in as a fight (below), and "the burden of violence is deferred, which we should make clear to the player."
- **No timing bars,** as for darts and pool: precise, with the right pushback.

## 1. The club and its history

### What's real

The island's coal was the first thing an empire took off it, and the men it brought to dig it were the first men it used up. Three facts carry the club:

1. **Fort Rupert, 1849 to 1850.** The Hudson's Bay Company brought Scottish colliers from Ayrshire to dig coal at Fort Rupert, on the island's north end. The Muirs among them. Within months they struck over their terms; two were put in irons, and most of them deserted for the California gold ([Fort Rupert](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fort_Rupert); [Andrew Muir, DCB](https://www.biographi.ca/en/bio/muir_andrew_8E.html)).
2. **The *Princess Royal*, 1854.** The Company's barque brought colliers and their families from Staffordshire to Nanaimo in November 1854 to dig the new seams ([The Princess Royal, BC Genesis](https://bcgenesis.uvic.ca/princess_royal.html)). The Company named a later ship the *Brierley Hill*, after the town many of them came from ([HBCA](https://mit.gov.mb.ca/chc/archives/_docs/hbca/ships_histories/brierley-hill.pdf)).
3. **Prize fighting is a crime in Canada and still is.** Section 83 of the Criminal Code makes it an offence to fight, second, promote or even attend a prize fight; since 2013 the only way round it is a contest the province sanctions ([s. 83](https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/acts/C-46/section-83.html); [S.C. 2013, c. 19](https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/AnnualStatutes/2013_19/page-1.html)). Everyone at a smoker is breaking the law by standing there.

### The story we tell (invented on top)

**The Friendly.** The colliers off the *Princess Royal* brought what pit towns in Staffordshire and Lancashire had: a friendly society (a sick-and-burial club every member paid into) and Sunday fighting, bare knuckles and "purring," which is two men in clogs holding each other by the shoulders and kicking each other's shins until one lets go. In the game, the first smoker on the island was in 1854, on the beach below the pit, a Staffordshire man against a Fort Rupert Scot who'd come down the coast with irons scars on his wrists. The Scot won. The purse went to a widow. That's the rule still: **a man who fights on a Sunday pays the widows' box,** win or lose, and the box has never been empty.

**It moved with whatever was using the island.** The pit (the Company, then the coal baron's mines), the cannery (1890s), and from 1952 the fish plant on the wharf, the floor where the fish were gutted, with its drains. The plant shut in the collapse. The Friendly kept the keys.

**Who runs it: nobody. The ring keeps itself** (settled 23 November with the survey thread, PR #16; Hutch left it to Claude). A **Timekeeper**, an old man on a herring barrel with a pocket watch, sets the card, calls the rounds and writes every result in **the ledger**: every bout since the colliers, its first pages in copperplate under the heading *The Friendly*. He keeps the widows' box. The Legion's old guard (the vets, `12_FROM_THE_DOOR.md`, story 7) are in the crowd and second whoever asks; the club (the bikers) runs the book on the side, which is where the money and a debt come from. Gary knows and doesn't go. The constable knows, and on a Sunday she's always somewhere else. Over the weeks the oldest names in the ledger turn out to be the men he fights.

**The rules are the London Prize Ring's,** the 1838 rules revised in 1853, the ones the colliers knew: a scratch line in the middle of the floor; a round ends when a man goes down; his corner has thirty seconds and then he has to walk up to the scratch on his own feet; no kicking, gouging, butting, biting or blows below the waist (purring nights excepted). No gloves, no clock. "Come up to scratch" and "up to scratch" are from these rules, and the old guard say them the way they were meant.

**Why they're dressed like that.** The Friendly's rule is that you fight "as the first men stood": moleskin trousers, braces, a collarless shirt or none, hair cropped or long, side whiskers if you've got them. The early opponents are town men in the old kit, a custom and a joke. It gets less like a costume each Sunday.

### The ladder: they get stranger

Smokers on Sundays: days 5, 12, 19 and 26 (from the survey). Eight names in the book, two bouts a card. The first is a man from town playing dress-up; by the last card nobody can say what he's fighting.

| Card | Opponent | How he fights | What's off about him |
|---|---|---|---|
| Day 5 | **Ox Pettigrew**, forklift at the co-op, in braces and a borrowed collar | a brawler: big swings, long wind-ups, no feet | nothing. He's sweet. His wife holds his glasses. (built: the first bout) |
| Day 5 | **Clem Harrow**, a purrer, clogs on | holds the shoulders and kicks shins: the legs go, then the balance | his clogs are older than the plant, and they fit |
| Day 12 | **The Ayrshire man** | old-style stance, left arm out, right at the chest; walks forward and never back | says it's 1850 and the Company owes him; irons scars on both wrists; nobody drove him here |
| Day 12 | **A stoker off a navy sloop out of Esquimalt** | a tidy London-rules boxer who throws you on a fall | the tattoos are a ship that was broken up in 1890 |
| Day 19 | **A special constable from the Big Strike, 1913** | fights like a man with a baton he isn't holding | the old guard won't look at him; one of them's grandfather was on the other side |
| Day 19 | **The man the print won't cut** | everything right and nothing in his face | the press can't take him: he's paper where his face should be |
| Day 26 | **The drowned man** | slow, cold, heavy, doesn't breathe, doesn't tire | he's wet. Every round. |
| Day 26 | **The champion: the last doorman** | fights the way the player fights (below) | his jacket's still in the Sov |

**The champion is the last doorman** (settled with the survey thread). He walked out of the Sov mid-rush on a Friday because the ring sent for him, and by the title fight on day 26 he looks like the men in the ledger's first pages. **He fights like the player.** Every live fight is logged (built: `FightSim.log`): which hand he leads with, what he throws when, where he stands, which way he slips, how often he grabs. The last doorman fights with that, his habits played back at him, a little better. The player who beats him beats himself.

**The empire, said once.** Each card's opponent is a man some empire used up on this island: the Company's colliers, the navy's stokers, the coal baron's specials, the cannery's drowned. The fish plant is the last of them and it's shut. The old guard don't explain it. One line, from the oldest, after the day 19 card, if the player asks why they keep doing it: "Every outfit that ever came here for something left us the men. Somebody has to take them on." (Toynbee's line that civilisations die by suicide, not murder, belongs to the paper's columnist, not to him.)

**Why fight.** Money (a purse, side bets, the club's book), standing (the Thursday paper's sports column runs the card, the rugby boys, the vets), voices (THE CORNER, the new voice of the ring, from the old guard who seconds him), and the practice (section 4). Losing costs his body for days, and the widows' box takes its cut either way.

## 2. The live fight

### What the player does (built)

First person. His own two fists at the bottom of the view, up in his guard. The other man's whole body in front of him, close.

| Input | What it does |
|---|---|
| **Left button, a stroke** | the right hand (his rear hand, the heavy one). Press on the fist, draw toward where it should land on the man, let go. |
| **Right button, a stroke** | the left hand (the lead: quicker, lighter). |
| The stroke's **shape** | straight = a straight punch; bowed to one side = a hook from that side; drawn upward = an uppercut. |
| The stroke's **speed and length** | how much he puts into it: more power, more of his weight over his feet, more breath, longer to get back. |
| **Press and hold still** on the man (a quarter second) | that hand takes hold where the pointer is: the wrist, the arm, the collar, the back of the neck, the belt. |
| **Drag while holding** | he moves the man's weight with it: pull, push, turn him. |
| **Drag in a circle while holding** | the circle (THE CIRCLE): the turn builds on itself until the man's feet can't keep up and he goes down where the arc ends. |
| **Both hands on the neck, from behind** | the choke. Each second held is a second; his hands on the arm say when he's gone. |
| **W A S D** | his feet: in, out, round. Range is everything. |
| **Q and E** | slip his head left or right. |
| **Space** | cover up: forearms by the face, chin down. The head's safe; the body isn't, and he can't see much. |
| **Esc** | in the ring: stay down / quit. On a story night it isn't there. |

### No bars, no numbers

No health bars, no meters, no timing marks. Everything the player needs is on the bodies and in the room:

- **Reading him.** Every blow he throws is telegraphed by his body: the shoulder drops, the weight goes back, the eyes go. A skilled man's tell is short; a drunk's is long. The player who watches the shoulder slips the punch. Wisdom lengthens what he sees (built: the tell grows with WIS), and the knack **the shoulder** draws it (section 4).
- **His breath** is in the sound (built: breathing that gets ragged) and the edges of the print closing in. Out of breath, his hands come down and his punches lose their snap.
- **Shock** is the print going soft and the sound going under water (built: `view.flash`, the heart, the muffle). A clean shot to the jaw lands as a held frame and a white flash; a man with his bell rung sways.
- **The other man's state** is on him: his guard dropping, his knees, his mouth open for air, his feet crossing.

### Why it feels like something (built)

- **Hit stop.** A clean blow holds the frame for 50 to 110 milliseconds by how hard it landed, then the head snaps. Blocks hold for less. The oldest trick in fighting games, and it's there because the player's hand needs to feel the contact.
- **Springs on the body.** Each blow is an impulse on the bones it lands near (the head, the chest, the hips), so a jab snaps the head and a hook turns it, and a body shot folds him. Nothing is a canned reaction; every hit lands from the angle it came.
- **His balance is real.** Each body has its weight over a base. Blows, pulls and his own big swings push it; his feet put it back. Past the edge he stumbles, past the next he's down. Big swings miss and carry him.
- **Mass.** A big man is slow to move and hard to stop. The ogre is three of you.
- **Sound** from the house kit: slaps, groans, the crowd, the boards.

### The sim (built: `godot/game/fight_sim.gd`)

Two bodies on the floor at 120 steps a second, the same seed giving the same fight. Each has a position and facing, a balance (weight over base) with its own spring, breath, shock, hurt by part (head, body, legs, arms), nerve, a guard (where the hands are, in the space of the other man's body), grips, and the action it's in (the tell, the blow travelling, the way back). The other man is the same sim run by a brain with a style (the brawler, the boxer, the purrer, the berserker), deciding ten times a second from what it can see.

Stats feed it: STR the force; DEX hand speed and accuracy; CON how much shock and breath he has; WIS how early he reads a tell; GUT the blow from the side he isn't watching; CHA the other man's nerve (and the crowd's). Drink, hurt and hunger come in as they do for checks.

## 3. Frozen seconds, tipping into live

Story fights keep the skeleton's frozen seconds (DECIDED 14 October): the press stops, prints the frame, and the choices are drawn on it in the voice's ink: **the circle** (an arc), **the blow** (a line), **the choke** (a loop), **off the line** (footprints) and **a word** (a ring at his mouth). Each has its dice, as checks do now.

New (DECIDED 23 November): some seconds end the freeze. A choice can be marked to **go live**: the print starts again and the fight carries on under the player's hands from exactly where the frame was. A live fight can be **frozen again** at its turning points: a knife, a third man, a fall, somebody offering help. The dice stay for those moments only: the first second, the fall, the count, the lock, the choke.

(built) `game/fight_seconds.gd` draws the frozen frame and its choices and rolls them; `game/fight.gd` runs the live part; a story fight is a script of both (`game/fight_cards.gd`).

## 4. Stats: what he practises becomes who he is

### The problem now

Seven stats from Gary's office, moved rarely by Ink. They're right as the spine, and they're too far from what the player does: throwing darts well doesn't make him any better at anything.

### Practice (built: `godot/engine/practice.gd`)

Under the seven, **practice**: tallies kept for what he actually does, named in the world's words, earned only by doing it well and never by doing it again and again.

| Practice | Earned by | Feeds |
|---|---|---|
| **Hands** | clean blows that land | STR, DEX |
| **Holds** | grips kept, men turned, walks to the door | STR, DEX |
| **Feet** | blows slipped by stepping, angles taken | DEX |
| **Chin** | taking it and staying up | CON |
| **Wind** | going rounds, the stairs, the cold | CON |
| **Read** | tells read: blows covered or slipped clean | WIS |
| **Sense** | the one he didn't see, felt anyway | GUT |
| **Nerve** | stepping in, standing up, a no that holds | CHA |
| **Arm** | darts (built: wired to good turns) | DEX |
| **Eye** | pool, the range | DEX, INT |
| **Faces** | the door: a licence caught, a lie heard | INT, WIS |

**No grinding,** by three rules (built): each practice can earn only so much in a night (a cap that a good night reaches); the same thing done the same way earns less each time (a man who only throws the right hand stops learning from it); and a win against someone worse than him teaches nothing.

**Thresholds.** Each practice has four marks (10, 25, 45 and 70 points). At each:

1. **A stat moves.** The first and third marks put a point on the stat it feeds (round-robin where it feeds two), never past 9 by practice alone (10 is earned in the story). The column says it, in a voice: THE FIGHTER after Hands, THE CORNER after Chin. (built)
2. **A knack.** The second and fourth marks give a knack, a named way of doing something that changes play. (built: the first eight below)

| Knack | From | What it does |
|---|---|---|
| **The short one** | Hands 2 | a short stroke hits with full weight: the six-inch punch |
| **Heavy hands** | Hands 4 | blows land with more shock |
| **The shoulder** | Read 2 | the other man's tell is drawn on him, a stroke of THE FIGHTER's ink at the shoulder |
| **Seen it before** | Read 4 | his tells last longer for you |
| **Hard head** | Chin 2 | the first clean shot of a fight doesn't ring the bell |
| **The circle closes** | Holds 2 | the circle takes less turning |
| **Second wind** | Wind 2 | once a fight, the breath comes back |
| **The angle** | Feet 2 | the sidestep is quicker and takes him off the line |
| **Steady hand** | Arm 2 | the dart's tremor halves (built in darts) |
| *(to come)* Nerve, Sense, Eye, Faces, and the fourth marks | | |

**Consequential.** Each knack is also an Ink variable (`knack_short_one`, `knack_the_shoulder`...), and each stat point is a line. So the story can see them: with **the shoulder**, the sucker punch at the bar (story 10) has a choice it didn't have, to see it coming; with a name in the ring (`ring_name`), the rugby boys stop testing him and start asking; with **hard head**, the elbow on the deck doesn't take his jaw. A stat that crosses 6 or 8 opens the passive reads the skeleton already promised (Wisdom 6: the friend at the rail stopped drinking).

**Loss.** A broken hand stops Hands for as long as it's broken; a week of drink takes from Wind; a concussion takes a point of INT until it heals. What's lost by the body comes back with the body, and the column says that too.

**On the page.** His notes get a page for his body: the seven, each with its practice under it in words ("Hands: getting quick"), the marks as pencil ticks, and the knacks as short entries in his own hand. (built: a first version of that page.)

## 5. The ogre on the deck (built as the first story fight)

From Hutch's door (`12_FROM_THE_DOOR.md`, 23 November), told nearly as it happened. A Friday or Saturday, late. Composite names; the man himself never gives a name.

1. **At the bar (frozen).** One of the servers comes running: someone's going crazy on the deck. Choices: go now; shout for Jim to call it in, then go (THE MANUAL); take the bat from under the bar (THE FIGHTER: and walk out with it in front of everyone).
2. **The deck (frozen).** A man seven feet tall is punching the deck boards; it sounds like thunder. Dale, a faller about his size, is hanging off one arm. Choices drawn on the frame: **a word** (he doesn't speak English; it does nothing, and the frame says so); **the hand on the shoulder** (he doesn't feel it); **take the free arm** (goes live); **the blow** (THE FIGHTER: the jaw; on a man this size you break your hand on it).
3. **Live: hold him.** You have his left arm, Dale has his right. He surges, and every surge drags you both. Keep him off the people at the rail and keep your feet: steer his weight against his surges. When he gets an arm free he hits the boards.
4. **Frozen: the sister.** A woman in a leather vest at the patio door: "You want my brothers?" Yes (five bikers come out, and the club has done the Sov's doorman a favour); no, we've got him (the next part is harder); tell her to call the cops (they come, far too late).
5. **Live: the walk.** Through the patio door, him roaring and pushing back. The door frame is the hard part.
6. **The tree.** Inside, he goes over on his face like a felled tree. Boom. He's up again as if nothing happened.
7. **Frozen: out.** Picked up and thrown out, and the elbow: a WIS or GUT roll, or the knack **the shoulder** sees it coming. Miss it and it's his jaw (hurt, a week of soup).
8. **After: the deferral.** Nobody comes. If she called, one car comes forty minutes later and asks where he went. The log. Gary's text in the morning. The jaw. Hutch: "The burden of violence is deferred." The player sees it plainly: the cost lands on whoever's at the door, and the law turns up after. The column says what it cost him, and nobody thanks him.
