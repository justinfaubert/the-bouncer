# Voices

How each person in the Sov talks, so that a line could be told apart from every other person's with the name covered up. [PROPOSED 2026-10-05, after Hutch: "The dialogue sounds the same across characters."]

Until now nearly everyone spoke the way the narration does: short, flat, understated declaratives, and a lot of them landed as rules ("One's fine. Nobody's fine on two."). The short hard rule is THE FATHER's register, and when everybody talks in rules, everybody sounds like him. The flat telling belongs to the narration and to the bouncer. Everybody else gets their own: their own business, their own rhythm, their own way of saying yes. The night stays understated; the people in it don't all have to be.

## Rules for everyone

- **The test:** cover the name. If the line could be anyone's, it isn't finished.
- **Each voice is a shape, not a gimmick.** Rhythm (how long the sentences run, where they break), vocabulary (work, age, place), what they do with a question, what they never say. One tic at most, used where it lands, not on every line.
- **Same facts.** A rewrite keeps every fact, number, name and promise the line carries, because choices and consequences hang off them. It changes how it's said.
- **About the same length.** The column is narrow. A voice can run on where that's the character, but nobody makes speeches.
- **The island.** Older people say "up island", "the mainland", "the ferry", "the mill", "the Esso", "a two-four", "a mickey", "eh". The old-timers still say "skookum" and "the chuck" (the sea). Kids talk like kids anywhere with a phone.
- **Hutch's lines stay word for word:** THE FIGHTER's read of the boyfriend ("He's had eight and he wants a ninth..."), the owner's "How is it tonight?", and the choice "Stand at the door." The `// HUTCH:` prompts are left for Hutch.
- **Serious, never quippy** (`01_PILLARS.md`). People can be funny the way people are; the writing never winks. No caricatures: each of them gets their best voice, and the game holds them dear.
- **Echoes on purpose stay echoes.** A rumour passed from mouth to mouth ("ten years on a door in the city"), the owner's date the bartender quotes back at him, and the voices he takes home (THE TABLE, THE KID) repeating what was said to him are meant to repeat.

## The staff

### THE BARTENDER
The lifer. Fifties. A server here at nineteen, thirty-one years behind this bar, and she drinks.
- **Talks while she works.** Her sentences get cut by the job ("Hang on." "Behind you.") and run on with *and* when she's on home ground. She counts and lists things.
- **Names:** customers are *hon* and *sweetheart*; that's the job. The doorman is *Door*, never his name and never *hon*, until he's lasted a week. The owner is *Junior* behind his back: she worked for his father.
- **Bar talk:** *eighty-sixed*, *comped*, *the well*, *the rail*, *walk-in*, *due backs*, *last call*.
- **She answers her own questions** ("How long? Thirty-one years."). She swears flatly, as punctuation, never at anyone ("the fuckin' card machine"). She never explains anything twice.
- **Never:** pep talks, wisecracks, or advice dressed up as advice. Advice from her is an order about the bar.
- *Was:* "Table 28 doesn't get cut off. Owner's rule." *Now:* "Table 28 don't get cut off. That's Junior's rule, so don't look at me."

### THE COOK
Props the kitchen door because the fryer runs hot, and the inspector costs him shifts.
- **The only fast talker.** Barked fragments, then a rant that comes out in one breath.
- **Kitchen talk:** *behind*, *in the weeds*, *the pass*, *walk-in*, *prep table*, *eighty-six*, *the line*. Temperatures and times.
- **Calls the doorman** *Security*. Swears inventively and constantly, mostly at objects.
- **Never** finishes a thought about himself.
- *Was:* "It's a hundred and ten in here." *Now:* "It's a hundred and ten on the line. A hundred and ten! It stays open or I cook."

### THE OWNER
On the phone, at dinner in town. Runs on the money code.
- **Everything turns into the money:** numbers, *busy*, *what I like to hear*. He never asks how anyone is.
- **Small-business talk:** *at the end of the day*, *moving forward*, *buddy*, *I don't need the how*. He says the inspector's date like a prayer.
- **Half-talks to the table he's at** ("No, the halibut. Sorry. Go.").
- **Opens with Hutch's "How is it tonight?"**, which means *how busy, how much*.
- **In person,** in his office (`content/ink/office.ink`): the same man, warmer, and he laughs at his own jokes before you do ("Ha. Okay. Ha. Jesus."). He talks like he's selling the job he's offering ("Come in, come in. Gary. Grab a seat."), says *Good. Good man.* to the answers he likes and *That's not a thing.* to the ones he doesn't, and passes the hard parts to the bookkeeper.
- *In the office:* "It's four o'clock somewhere. It's four o'clock here."

### THE BOOKKEEPER
Gary's father's, and then Gary's. Forty-one years in March, and before that the bank across the road.
- **The fewest words in the game,** all of them polite and none of them warm. Numbers, procedure, and a fact dropped while looking at her screen ("Doug says a big fellow with hair closed the Legion last night.").
- **Corrects Gary without looking up** ("Six hundred and fifty. A month. With the hydro.").
- *Thank you so much.* means the opposite. *Mm-hm.* means she's written it down.
- **Never:** small talk, his name, or an opinion she'd have to stand behind.

### Gary's voices: THE TILL and SENIOR
Heard only in Gary's office, in his head, the way the doorman's voices will be heard in his. They're Gary's, never the doorman's (`content/data/voices.yaml`, `whose: gary`).
- **THE TILL,** in typewriter: what things cost and what they'll cost. Prices, counts and sums, and nothing else ("Eleven dollars a shirt. Forty-eight shirts."). It never says what it thinks of anyone except as money ("Long hair. That's a complaint a week.").
- **SENIOR,** in tall capitals: Gary's father, who bought the Sov in 1958 and is dead. Short judgements of men, from a long time behind a bar, and of his son, whom he calls *Junior* ("YOUR MOTHER IRONED YOURS TILL YOU WERE THIRTY, JUNIOR."). His rules are the bar's rules ("That's my rule.") and he's usually right about people and wrong about everything else.

### TERRY
The hotel's night clerk, on a stool behind the desk, watching the bar's TV through a propped door.
- **Chatty and lonely.** Asks the score before anything else, and talks hockey to anyone.
- **Fussy about procedure** that nobody else cares about. Says *eh* a lot.
- *Was:* "Something wrong?" *Now:* "Oh, hey. Something wrong? What's the score?"

## The regulars

### TABLE 28 (the hive)
Five high-functioning boomer alcoholics, every day but Sunday. One voice most of the time.
- **A chorus.** Exclamations, toasts, one of them finishing another's line, a joke repeated louder because it worked.
- **They talk about him in front of him**, in the third person, with nicknames: *the new kid*, *big fella*, *son*.
- **Boomer and mill talk:** *back in the day*, *you couldn't do that now*, *when the mill was three shifts*, *the Island*, *up island*.
- **Their code is loyalty, generosity, and we were here first.** Generous loudly, offended loudly.
- *Was:* "He's all right." *Now:* "He's all right, the new kid! Isn't he? He's all right!"

### Table 28, one at a time
- **SCRATCH:** scratch tickets all afternoon; feeds the rat, Walter. Talks in luck: *a sign*, *due*, *you've got to be in it*. Reads everything as an omen and gives the odds on it.
- **CARDIGAN:** the quiet one, retired from the mill office with a pension. Whole, careful sentences; asks a question and waits for the answer, which nobody else at the table does. Sad, and polite about it.
- **GLASSES (Dennis):** drunk and fond. Says your name too often, and loses the end of his sentences.
- **HEARING AID** repeats the last word anyone says, one word, a beat late. Turns the aid down when he disagrees.
- **TEAM JACKET** is the table's mouth when it isn't talking as one: retired, loud, a teller. Everything comes out as a story with himself in it, and a line that worked gets said again. He needles the doorman and calls him *son* and *big fella*; he ribs Dennis whether Dennis is there or not. Under it he keeps the table's books: who sat, who bought, who was last. He's funnier than he is kind, until the table's threatened, and then he goes quiet and exact.

### END OF THE BAR
The man who seems to speak for the rats. Nobody saw him come in. He was coming in before the bartender started.
- **No contractions, ever.** He is the only person in the building who talks this way.
- **Formal and courteous, an old storyteller's voice:** *one hears*, *I would be obliged*, *as it happens*, *the terms*. He calls the doorman *Doorman*.
- **The cannery, the tide, the pilings and the dock** are in his mouth as if they were last week. He says *we* and leaves it to you who he means.
- *Was:* "They built the hotel on the pilings and the pilings are still there. So's everything that lived in them." *Now:* "They built the hotel on the pilings, and the pilings are still there. So is everything that lived in them."

### SLIPPERS
A resident from upstairs, room nine, down in her slippers.
- **Talks to the doorman like a grandson who's let her down,** and calls him *dear*.
- **Specific about everything**: the room number, the hour, what her late husband would have done.

## The kids

### HOODIE
Nineteen, earbuds, hasn't slept. A photo of his licence on his phone.
- **Lowercase, barely punctuated, mumbled.** Short, then a run-on that gives up halfway: *or whatever*, *like*, *i mean*.
- **Precise about small injustices** ("it's literally in the truck"). *bro* only when he's cornered.
- **Now and then a whole true sentence**, flat, when he's too tired to stop it.
- **Never** shouts, and never says what he means first.

### THE DRIVER
His sister. Twenty-two, sober, the one with the keys. The adult in her family.
- **Full sentences, capitals and full stops.** Organized, as if she's reading off a list she made in the truck.
- **Times and facts:** "since six this morning", "two minutes". She repeats the key fact the same way every time, because she's had to say it to too many people.
- **Manners as a lever:** *please*, *thank you*, said hard. Says *Okay.* to close a subject, and *Kyle.* as a whole sentence.
- **Never** swears at the doorman. Swears at Kyle.

### VAPE (Kyle)
Her boyfriend. Twenty, drunk before he got here, the one who'll swing.
- **Lowercase like the kids, but loud:** more words than Hoodie ever uses, and he capitalises the word he's shouting.
- **Overfamiliar:** *bro*, *my guy*, *big dog*, *chief*. Everything is a negotiation or a grievance, and he quotes the law at you ("that's literally illegal").
- **Never** knows when it's over.

## Around the pool table

### THE BIG MAN
At the pool table since before the shift, playing his buddy for money and letting him win. The kids' father.
- **Slow, genial, deflecting.** A working man's island talk: *eh*, *bud*, *the mill*, *a fella*.
- **Calls the doorman *bud*,** out of habit. He says *my girl* and *the boy* about his own, never their names.
- **Talks to the balls and the table** when he doesn't want to talk to you ("Sit. Sit down.").
- **When something lands, he goes to two words.**
- *Was:* "Rough week." *Now:* "Ah, it's been a week, bud. One of them weeks."

### THE BUDDY
Plays pool with the big man. Small, quick, nervous.
- **Fast patter that doubles up:** *yeah yeah*, *no no no*, *listen*. Apologises for the big man before anyone's complained.

### GOOD SHOES
In the 86 binder. Polite, sober, well dressed. Says the owner lifted his ban.
- **Polished and too reasonable.** Long, smooth, correct sentences, and *I completely understand*.
- **Drops the owner's name the way other people show a card.** Calls the doorman *friend*.
- **The menace is in the same calm voice,** never louder.

## Up the highway

### THE ESSO KID
Twenty, on nights at the Esso, in a toque and the Esso's vest, on the ice freezer by the door with his phone. Physics between customers, and a gig rating a machine's answers.
- **Talks like a kid,** *man*, *like*, *kind of*, and then says one exact thing about time or the sky ("Planes blink. Satellites move. It was there last night, same place, same time.").
- **Asks you to decide things** and tells you after that there was nothing to decide ("They're both it, by the way. There's no person one.").
- **Never** looks at you while the thing in the sky is there. Numbers come out precise and offhand ("Four cents an answer. I've done like eleven thousand.").

## Passing through

- **RAINCOAT** (Dennis's wife): tired and exact. She knows where he sits. Short, dry, and she's done this for thirty years.
- **THE CONSTABLE:** procedure and fatigue. *Okay. So.* *Walk me through it.* *I've got one car for the whole valley.* Writes while you talk.
- **A SMOKER on the deck:** small-town gossip, sourced: *I heard*, *my brother-in-law says*, *they're saying*.
- **HI-VIS:** a mill-contract worker in room eleven, four beers in. Few words, all of them tired, and the island's working greeting (*How's she goin'.*). Says what he's owed plainly: he pays rent here.

## The word on him

What a group says about him when something lands (`content/rules/word.yaml`) is in that group's voice too, so the stamp under a line can be told apart with the group's name covered: table 28 exclaims about *the new kid* and *big fella* in the third person ("Hear, hear!", "Boo! Not one of ours."); the kids are lowercase, like the group chat it'll end up in ("narc", "he's actually chill"); the bartender is a word or a count ("Yep.", "Ten doormen, and this one."); the owner turns it into money ("That's money out the door, buddy."); the cook says *Heard.* or *Every goddamn time.*

## The voices in his head

They already have their own type, colour and job, so they keep their registers: **THE FATHER**'s short door rules; **THE MANUAL**'s course binder (*the licensee*, *may be held liable*); **THE WRONG MAN**'s retold story (*in the story*); **THE FIGHTER**'s body read, blunt and physical. They're not part of this pass.
